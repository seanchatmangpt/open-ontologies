# Builder Registry — Nehemiah 52 Operating Grammar

This registry documents every named builder recorded in Nehemiah 3, their gate
assignment, their wall section, and their architectural role in the operating grammar.
The registry is the operational surface of the `bos:MusterLedger`. Every entry here
corresponds to one or more `bos:Builder` individuals in `ontology/nehemiah-52.ttl`.

**Doctrine:** Anonymity is an anti-pattern. Every builder must be named, gate-assigned,
and section-covenanted before work may begin. An unnamed entry in this registry is
not a valid muster record.

Prefix: `bos: <https://open-ontologies.org/bible-o-star#>`

## Extended Builder Entries

### Eliashib the High Priest

**bos: Individual:** `bos:EliashibBuilder`
**Gate:** Sheep Gate
**Section:** Neh. 3:1 — Sheep Gate, Tower of the Hundred (Meah), Tower of Hananel
**Role in architecture:** Eliashib holds the dedication authority. The Sheep Gate
consecration is the precondition for every other gate's work. No work lifecycle may
begin without a dedication marker traceable to Eliashib's consecration event.
As high priest, he is the only party authorized to issue `bos:DedicationMarker`
instances. Any payload claiming dedication authority from another party is refused
as `bos:FabricatedDedication` at the Sheep Gate.

---

### Hanun + Inhabitants of Zanoah

**bos: Individual:** `bos:HanunBuilder`
**Gate:** Valley Gate
**Section:** Neh. 3:13 — Valley Gate + one thousand cubits to Dung Gate (longest assignment)
**Role in architecture:** Hanun's one-thousand-cubit covenant is the canonical lower-bound
reference. It is the largest single section in Nehemiah 3, and its scale demonstrates
that lower-bound constraints are not minimalist — they may be the largest assignments
on the wall. Hanun's covenant is the counter-receipt against fear payloads that attempt
to compress scope.

---

### Meremoth Son of Uriah

**bos: Individual:** `bos:MeremothBuilder`
**Gate:** Fish Gate (primary); Fish Gate area (second section, Neh. 3:21)
**Sections:** Neh. 3:4 (Fish Gate approach — section from Fish Gate to house of Eliashib); Neh. 3:21 (from door of Eliashib's house to the end of his house — geographically near Eliashib's house on the north wall, not at the Horse Gate on the east wall)
**Role in architecture:** Meremoth is the canonical double-assignment builder. Nehemiah
3:21 explicitly records his second assignment near Eliashib's house. This is lawful under the operating grammar
provided both sections carry independent Gate Covenants. Meremoth demonstrates that a
builder's accountability is not split by a double assignment — both sections are fully
receipted under his name. Note: the TTL correctly assigns Meremoth to `bos:FishGate` only; his second section (3:21) is in the Fish Gate / north wall area adjacent to Eliashib's house, not at the Horse Gate (Neh. 3:28) on the east wall.

---

### Malkijah Son of Rechab

**bos: Individual:** `bos:MalkijahRechabBuilder`
**Gate:** Dung Gate
**Section:** Neh. 3:14 — Dung Gate structure
**Role in architecture:** Malkijah governs the refutation boundary. He does not build
new structures — he removes unsound ones. The Dung Gate is the only gate whose primary
admission criterion is confirmed unsoundness. Malkijah's accountability is to the
receipt archive of unsoundness, not to a construction ledger.

---

### Shallun Son of Col-Hozeh

**bos: Individual:** `bos:ShallunBuilder`
**Gate:** Fountain Gate
**Section:** Neh. 3:15 — Fountain Gate + Pool of Siloah wall + stairs descending to
City of David
**Role in architecture:** Shallun governs reference resolution. The Pool of Siloah is a
water source — a coordination resource. Shallun's extended section (gate + pool wall +
stairs) mirrors the cross-reference coordination function: the Fountain Gate does not
merely admit a single artifact type, it links structures across the wall circuit. This
agent is the Fountain Gate operator.

---

### Nehemiah the Governor

**bos: Individual:** `bos:NehemiahGovernorBuilder`
**Gate:** Inspection Gate (swarm conductor, not section builder)
**Section:** Neh. 3:31-32 — Inspection Gate approach (via goldsmiths and merchants;
Nehemiah's own administrative role covers the Inspection Gate itself)
**Role in architecture:** Nehemiah is the only party who may emit `bos:InspectionReceipt`
individuals. He does not hold a section assignment in the same sense as other builders;
his accountability is to the completeness of the circuit. He inspects the wall at night
before assigning builders (Neh. 2:13-15), and he alone seals the ALIVE verdict. Any
ALIVE claim not traceable to Nehemiah's Inspection Gate is a `bos:SelfCertifiedALIVE`
defect.

---

### Shemaiah Son of Shecaniah

**bos: Individual:** `bos:ShemaiahBuilder`
**Gate:** East Gate
**Section:** Neh. 3:29 — East Gate structure
**Role in architecture:** Shemaiah is the East Gate keeper. He governs forward-facing
change governance. Notably, Neh. 6:10-13 records that a different Shemaiah (son of
Delaiah) attempted to lure Nehemiah into the temple under a false threat — this is the
canonical `bos:FearPayload` / `bos:InvitationTrap` pattern. The East Gate builder
Shemaiah (son of Shecaniah) is distinct from the false-prophet Shemaiah and must not
be conflated.

## Registry Completeness

All builders in Nehemiah 3:1-32 with a named section assignment are documented above.
Two categories of entries are explicitly present in the muster:

- **Absent builders:** Neh. 3:5 records the Tekoite nobles "did not put their necks
  to the work of their Lord." They are registered as absent, not omitted.
- **Daughters:** Neh. 3:12 records Shallum's daughters repaired their section.
  They are present in the muster by the family name.

An absent entry is a valid muster record. The absence is the receipt.
