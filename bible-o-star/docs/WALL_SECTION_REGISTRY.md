# Wall Section Registry — Nehemiah 52 Operating Grammar

This registry documents the wall sections from Nehemiah 3 and maps each to its
bounded work unit, the accountable builder, and the nearest gate boundary. Wall
sections are the canonical unit of architectural accountability. Every bounded
work unit in the operating grammar is modeled on a wall section: it has a defined
extent, a named builder, a gate covenant, and a receipt chain.

**Doctrine:** A wall section is a bounded work unit — not a milestone, not a sprint,
not a deliverable category. It has exact endpoints (often described in Nehemiah by
the names of adjacent houses, towers, or landmarks), a named accountable builder,
and a single primary gate assignment.

Prefix: `bos: <https://open-ontologies.org/bible-o-star#>`

## Wall Section to Artifact Bounded-Work-Unit Mapping

In the operating grammar, a wall section models a bounded work unit (BWU). The
table below defines the canonical attributes of a BWU derived from the wall section pattern.

| Attribute | Wall Section Source | BWU Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Named extent | "From X to Y" (Neh. 3 landmark references) | Declared scope with measurable start and end points |
| Named builder | Every section has a named assignee | Every BWU has a named accountable agent |
| Gate covenant | Each section is within a gate's operating zone | Every BWU is covenanted at its primary gate |
| Receipt chain | Work → building act → Receipt | BWU → manufacturing act → Receipt |
| Double assignment | Meremoth, Meshullam (two sections each) | Double BWU ownership is lawful with independent covenants |
| Absence record | Tekoite nobles (Neh. 3:5) | Absent accountable agent is a visible muster entry, not a silent omission |
| Longest section | Hanun's one thousand cubits | The largest BWU is not split unless independently covenanted |
| Closed circuit | Section 37 connects back to Section 1 | All BWUs close the circuit — no open-ended section |

## Key Structural Properties

### The Closed Circuit

The wall circuit in Nehemiah 3 begins and ends at the Sheep Gate. The last entry
(Neh. 3:32, goldsmiths and merchants) covers the section "from the upper room of
the corner to the Sheep Gate." This closure is architectural: an open-ended wall
is not a wall. The operating grammar requires that every receipt chain closes — no
BWU may declare ALIVE with an open endpoint.

### The Double Assignment Pattern

Meremoth and Meshullam each have two wall sections documented in Nehemiah 3. The
grammar treats this as lawful when both sections are independently covenanted and
separately receipted. A double assignment does not split accountability — both
sections are fully under the builder's name.

### The Absence Record

Neh. 3:5 records that the Tekoite nobles "did not put their necks to the work of
their Lord" — they were present in the muster zone but refused the work. Their
absence is recorded with the same precision as a completion. A muster that cannot
record absence is a press release, not a ledger. The same Tekoites completed a
second section (Neh. 3:27), indicating that the working Tekoites are distinct from
the nobles — and that both entries appear in the registry.

### Nehemiah's Night Inspection

Before any builder assignments were made, Nehemiah conducted a night inspection of
the wall (Neh. 2:13-15). He inspected the circuit alone, without naming what he
was doing. This pre-assignment inspection is the source of the Inspection Gate's
authority: the gate operator knows the full circuit before any single section is
covenanted. No wall section exists in isolation; all sections are evaluated against
the whole.

## bos: WallSection Individuals (Summary)

All 37 wall section individuals are declared in `ontology/nehemiah-52.ttl` as
instances of `bos:WallSection`. Key triples pattern:

```turtle
bos:ValleyGateSectionWS a bos:WallSection ;
  rdfs:label "Valley Gate Section (one thousand cubits)" ;
  bos:hasCanonicalReference "Neh.3.13" ;
  bos:hasBuilder bos:HanunBuilder ;
  bos:assignedToGate bos:ValleyGate ;
  rdfs:comment "Hanun + inhabitants of Zanoah; one thousand cubits from Valley Gate to Dung Gate (Neh.3.13)." .
```

Full TTL declarations are in `ontology/nehemiah-52.ttl` under the `BUILDER INSTANCES`
and `WALL SECTION INSTANCES` sections.
